# World

## The short version

Ashfall is set sixty-one years after the Water Wars. The wars shattered the institutions that once moved clean water, food, fuel, and people across the region. Settlements now survive by repairing old infrastructure, trading carefully, and deciding who they can afford to trust.

Sex-selective attrition during the wars, followed by the Rust Fever male die-off, left the region at roughly ten women for every man. The number is a social fact, not a promise of safety or prosperity: communities have different histories, rules, and attitudes, and scarcity shapes daily life for everyone.

The protagonist is a mutant: 2.05 metres tall, amber-eyed, with a chitin-plated left arm and a body that is extraordinarily difficult to kill. He is also the last reliable father in the region. That makes him politically consequential—factions want influence over his choices, his alliances, and what the future of the population might look like. His value to others must not erase his agency or the agency of the people around him.

## Tone and limits

The voice is hard and dry, with warmth underneath. People use practical humour, remember what things cost, and show care through work as often as through speeches. Hope is possible, but it is built and maintained rather than granted.

This is grounded post-apocalyptic science fiction. There is **no magic and no zombie outbreak**. Mutations and disease belong to the material world; the setting should not use supernatural explanations to solve ordinary problems. Rust Fever is a historical catastrophe, not a convenient monster premise.

## Story principles

- Treat water, food, medicine, power, shelter, and trust as real constraints.
- Make political pressure emerge from people and institutions responding to scarcity—not from the protagonist's biology alone.
- Keep intimacy, family, and reproduction personal and consensual; do not frame anyone as a resource or a reward.
- Let small acts of competence and kindness matter without softening the world's material stakes.
